Why choose a 48-month auto loan?

A 48-month (4-year) auto loan is a solid middle ground. Shorter than a 5- or 6-year loan, you pay less total interest and typically avoid owing more than the car is worth for too long. The trade-off: a higher monthly payment than a 60-month term. Use this calculator to see if the payment fits your budget.

Example: $25,000 at 6.5% for 48 months

A $25,000 auto loan at 6.5% for 48 months gives a monthly payment of about $593. Over four years you’d pay roughly $28,450 in total—$3,450 in interest. The same loan stretched to 60 months would drop the payment to about $489, but total interest would rise to roughly $4,350—about $900 more. Use the calculator above to compare your own numbers.

48 months vs 60 months: what to consider

  • Monthly budget — A 48-month loan means a higher payment. If it strains your budget, a 60-month term may be more manageable, though you’ll pay more interest.
  • Total cost — Shorter terms cost less in interest. On a $20,000 loan at 7%, going from 60 to 48 months saves about $700 in interest.
  • Depreciation — Cars lose value quickly. A 48-month loan helps you stay ahead of depreciation and reduces the risk of negative equity if you sell or trade early.

Frequently asked questions

Is 48 months a good auto loan term?
For many borrowers, yes. It balances a manageable payment with less total interest than longer terms. If the payment is too high, 60 months is a common alternative.
How much is a $30,000 car loan for 48 months?
At 6.5%, about $712/month. At 8%, about $739/month. Use the calculator above for your exact rate.
Can I change the term to 36 or 60 months?
Yes. The term field is editable. Change it to compare different loan lengths.
Does this work for used cars?
Yes. The math is the same. Used car rates are often higher—enter the rate your lender quotes.
