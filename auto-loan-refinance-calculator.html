Why use an auto refinance calculator

Use this free tool to see whether refinancing your car loan saves money. Enter your current balance and rate, then try a lower rate—often available if your car loan was taken when rates were higher or your credit has improved.

Compare current vs new rate

If you owe $25,000 at 9% over 48 months, you pay about $622/month. Refinance to 6% with the same term and your payment drops to ~$587—saving over $1,600 in total interest. Use our interest calculator for deeper comparisons.

Check if savings outweigh fees

Some lenders charge refinance or title fees. A small fee may still be worth it if the rate drop is big enough. Run the numbers here, then get a real quote from banks and credit unions.

Stress-test different terms

Refinancing to a shorter term can cut total interest but raise your payment. Use our extra payments calculator to model payoff scenarios. For new car shopping, see our car loan calculator or used car loan calculator.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount — Your current remaining balance.
  2. Enter the interest rate — Your current rate, then recalculate with a lower rate to compare.
  3. Enter the term in months — Remaining months, or a new term if you’re changing it.
  4. Click Calculate — See monthly payment and total interest. Compare runs to see savings.

What affects your payment

  • Interest rate — Your rate depends on credit, lender, and loan type. Shop multiple offers.
  • Loan term — Shorter terms mean higher payments but less total interest. Longer terms spread the cost.
  • Loan amount — The principal directly affects both monthly payment and total interest.

Frequently asked questions

Is this estimate accurate?
This shows principal and interest. Your lender may round differently or charge fees. Use it for planning; confirm with your lender.
Can I compare different terms?
Yes—change the term in months to see how 48 vs 60 vs 72 months affects your payment and total interest.
When does refinancing a car loan make sense?
If rates dropped since you borrowed, or your credit improved, you may save. Compare current payment vs new rate here—and factor in any refinance fees.
Will refinancing hurt my credit?
A hard inquiry and new account may briefly lower your score. For most people, the savings outweigh a small, temporary dip if the rate drop is meaningful.
Can I refinance with negative equity?
Some lenders allow it if you owe more than the car’s value, but terms may be tougher. Get quotes from credit unions and online lenders.
