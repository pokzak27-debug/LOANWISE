Why 60 months is the most popular auto loan term

A 60-month (5-year) auto loan is the standard choice for many US car buyers. It spreads the cost over five years, keeping the monthly payment lower than a 48- or 36-month loan. The trade-off: you pay more total interest and may owe more than the car is worth for longer. Use this calculator to see if the payment fits your budget and what you’d save with a shorter term.

Example: $28,000 at 6.5% for 60 months

A $28,000 auto loan at 6.5% for 60 months gives a monthly payment of about $548. Over five years you’d pay roughly $32,900 in total—$4,900 in interest. The same loan over 48 months would raise the payment to about $664, but total interest would drop to roughly $3,850—saving over $1,000. Use the calculator above to compare your own scenario.

60 months vs 48 months: pros and cons

  • Lower payment — A 60-month term means a smaller monthly payment, which can help if you’re stretching your budget.
  • More interest — You pay more interest over the life of the loan. On a $25,000 loan at 7%, the difference between 48 and 60 months is about $900 in extra interest.
  • Negative equity — Cars depreciate fast. With a 60-month loan, you may owe more than the car is worth for the first few years, especially if you put little down.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 60-month car loan a good idea?
It depends on your budget. A 60-month loan keeps payments lower but costs more in interest. If you can afford a higher payment, a 48-month loan saves money.
How much is a $30,000 car payment for 60 months?
At 6.5%, about $587/month. At 8%, about $608/month. Use the calculator above for your exact rate.
Can I pay off a 60-month loan early?
Usually yes. Most US auto lenders don’t charge prepayment penalties. Paying extra each month cuts interest and payoff time.
What’s the difference between 60 and 72 months?
72 months stretches the loan another year—lower payment but more total interest. Use the calculator and change the term to compare.
