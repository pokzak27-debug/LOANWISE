What drives your monthly payment?

Three numbers control your car loan monthly payment: the amount you borrow (principal), the annual interest rate, and the loan term in months. A higher amount or rate raises the payment. A longer term spreads the cost over more months, so each payment drops—but total interest usually increases.

Lenders apply interest to the balance you still owe each month. Early on, more of your payment goes to interest. Over time, more goes to principal until the loan is paid off.

Typical terms and why they matter

Car loans often run 36, 48, 60, or 72 months. A 48-month loan has higher payments than 60 months but typically costs less in total interest. A 72-month loan keeps payments low but stretches repayment and can leave you underwater—owing more than the car is worth—if you sell or trade early.

Use the calculator to compare. Try 48 vs 60 months at the same amount and rate. See how much extra interest the longer term adds, and decide if the lower payment is worth it.

Getting an accurate estimate

Enter the net amount you finance after down payment and trade-in. If the dealer rolls taxes or fees into the loan, include them. The rate should match what your lender quoted—APR, not a promotional teaser unless that’s the rate for your full term.

Frequently asked questions

How is the monthly payment calculated?
We use the standard amortization formula: each payment covers interest on the remaining balance plus principal. The formula is M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is principal, r is the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12), and n is the number of payments.
Why does my bank’s payment differ slightly?
Lenders may round differently or use a different day-count. Our result should be very close—within a few cents for most loans.
Can I calculate a 0% promotional loan?
Yes. Enter 0 for the interest rate. Your monthly payment is simply the loan amount divided by the number of months.
Does this work for leases?
No. Leases use residual value and a money factor. This tool is for traditional installment car loans.
