How your monthly loan payment is calculated

When you borrow money, you repay it in fixed monthly installments. Each payment splits into interest (the cost of borrowing) and principal (the amount you owe). Early on, most of your payment goes to interest; by the end, almost everything goes to principal. Three numbers control the result: loan amount, annual rate, and term in months. Raise the amount or rate and the payment goes up; stretch the term and the payment usually falls—but you often pay more interest in total. Real-world example

Suppose you borrow $15,000 at 7.5% for 48 months. Your monthly payment would be about $363. Over four years you’d pay roughly $17,400 in total—$2,400 of that is interest. If you extended the same loan to 60 months, the payment drops to around $300, but total interest rises to about $3,000. Use the calculator above to plug in your own numbers and compare.

What affects your loan payment

  • Loan amount (principal) — Borrowing more increases both your monthly payment and total interest.
  • Interest rate — A higher rate raises the payment and the total cost. Even a 1% difference can add hundreds or thousands over the life of the loan.
  • Loan term — A longer term lowers the monthly payment but usually means more interest paid over time.
  • Fees — Origination or processing fees can be rolled into the loan, effectively increasing the amount you repay.

Understanding your results

Monthly payment
The amount due each period until the loan is paid off. On a standard fixed-rate loan, this stays steady so you can budget.
Total payment
Everything you will send the lender over the full term: original principal plus all interest, in this model.
Total interest
The cost of borrowing—the gap between total payment and the amount you first borrowed.

Frequently asked questions

What affects my monthly loan payment?
Principal, annual rate, and term in months. Change any one and the payment changes. Small rate moves matter more on large balances and long terms.
Does this include bank fees or insurance?
No. We model principal and interest only. If your lender bundles fees into the loan, add them to the amount you enter; otherwise budget fees separately.
Can I use 0% interest?
Yes. Enter 0 for the rate. The payment is approximately the loan amount divided by the number of months, before any real-world fees.
Is the result a guarantee from a lender?
No. It is a mathematical estimate. Your bank may round differently or apply promotions you have not entered here.
How is this different from a credit card minimum?
Cards revolve and minimums can stretch repayment for years. This tool assumes a fixed payoff date like a typical term loan.
